Explore Kenya

Rift valley lakes, red-dust savanna, and coastline warmed by the Indian Ocean — Kenya packs an entire continent's worth of landscape into one country.

Top Destinations

Three places worth building a trip around.

Maasai Mara

Home to the Great Migration, where over a million wildebeest cross the Mara River each year, chased by crocodiles and watched over by resident lions.

Diani Beach

Fourteen kilometers of white sand along the south coast, backed by palm forest, with reef diving and dhow sailing just offshore.

Mount Kenya

Africa's second-highest peak, ringed by bamboo forest and alpine moorland, with routes that need no technical climbing experience.

Things to Do

A few ways to fill the days.

Game Drives

Early-morning and late-afternoon drives across the Mara or Amboseli, when the light is soft and the animals are most active.

Snorkeling & Diving

Marine parks off Diani and Watamu protect coral reefs just a short boat ride from shore, home to turtles and reef fish.

Mountain Trekking

Multi-day routes up Mount Kenya pass through five distinct climate zones in a single ascent, from rainforest to glacier.

Travel Information

The practical details.

Best Time to Visit

July through October covers the dry season and the Great Migration; January and February offer calving season with fewer crowds.

Accommodation

Choose from tented safari camps in the reserves, beachfront lodges on the coast, or city hotels in Nairobi as a base for connections.

Getting Around

Domestic flights link Nairobi to the major parks and the coast in under an hour; road transfers take longer but show more of the country.

Travel Tips

A yellow fever certificate is required for entry from certain countries, and most safari camps arrange park permits on your behalf.

However you split your time between savanna, mountain, and coast, Kenya rewards travelers who leave room in the schedule to stay a day longer than planned.
